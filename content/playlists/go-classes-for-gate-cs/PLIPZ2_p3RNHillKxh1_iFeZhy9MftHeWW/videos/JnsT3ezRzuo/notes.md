## Concepts extracted

- Mathematical logic splits into propositional logic and first-order logic; this lecture covers propositional logic only.
- Propositional logic is described as the simplest logic and as a close relative of Boolean algebra.
- Every statement in propositional logic is either true or false - 'a world of true, false'.
- A proposition is a declarative statement that is either true or false, but not both.
- Declarative means it declares a fact; the fact may be true or false and it is still a proposition.
- 'Proposition' and 'statement' are used as synonyms.
- Examples worked in the lecture: 3 + 2 = 4 (a proposition, false), 6 < 24 (a proposition, true), 'triangles have four vertices' (a proposition, false), 'tomorrow is my birthday' (a proposition once the speaker and day are fixed), 'this rose is white' (a proposition once the context is fixed).
- A statement is judged a proposition only after its context (speaker, time, place, object) is fixed.
