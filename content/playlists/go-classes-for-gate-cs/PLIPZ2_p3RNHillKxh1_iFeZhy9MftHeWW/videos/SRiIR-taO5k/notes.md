## Concepts extracted

- Three ways to settle a propositional question: truth table, by-case method, simplification.
- The instructor calls the truth table boring, time-consuming and inefficient, and stops using it for practice questions.
- By-case method: a propositional variable is either true or false, so two cases cover every row of the truth table for that variable.
- For two variables there are four rows, but only two cases are needed when the expression is split on one variable.
- The same argument extends to three variables: cases on one variable cover all eight rows.
- The method is presented as analytical and fast, which is why it is preferred for GATE questions.
