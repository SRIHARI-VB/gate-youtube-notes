Signed Area and the Running Total

Objective. See what a definite integral measures: sweep a boundary from the lower limit to the upper one and watch the area accumulate with a sign. Then see the same quantity as a function of its upper limit, and see why its slope is the height of the curve - the fundamental theorem of calculus.
1.00x

Static explanation

Take f(x) = x squared minus 1 on the interval from 0 to 2. The definite integral from 0 to b of f is the limit of a sum of thin rectangles as their width shrinks, and the number it produces is the net signed area: pieces above the x-axis count positive, pieces below count negative.

The curve crosses the axis at x = 1. From 0 to 1 the integrand is negative, and the integral of x squared minus 1 there is one third minus one, which is minus two thirds. From 1 to 2 the integrand is positive, and the integral is two thirds plus two thirds, which is four thirds. Adding the pieces, the integral from 0 to 2 is two thirds. The geometric area between the curve and the axis over the same interval is the sum of the absolute values, two thirds plus four thirds, which is 2 - a different number, and that difference is exactly what a modulus question is asking about.

Now read the same integral as a function of its upper limit: F(b) is the integral from 0 to b. Then F(0) = 0, F(1) = minus two thirds and F(2) = two thirds, and F is zero again at the square root of 3, because the negative piece has been exactly paid back by then. Differentiating F gives x squared minus 1 back, which is f. So the slope of the running total at each point equals the height of the curve there: at x = 0 the height is minus 1 and the slope is minus 1, at x = 1 the height is 0 and the slope is 0, at x = 2 the height is 3 and the slope is 3. That statement is part one of the fundamental theorem of calculus, and it is why a definite integral can be computed from any antiderivative.

Finally, the integrand is even, so the integral from minus 1 to 1 equals twice the integral from 0 to 1, which is minus four thirds. An even integrand doubles over a symmetric interval; an odd one cancels.

Common misconception

Reading the running total as the area. For this curve the area from 0 to 2 is 2 while the integral is two thirds, and at b = 1 the integral is negative although no area can be negative. The integral is a signed quantity; the area is the integral of the modulus.

Quick check

What is the integral of x squared minus 1 from 0 to 1, and what is the geometric area of the same region?

Answer

The integral is minus two thirds and the area is two thirds. An antiderivative is x cubed over 3 minus x, so at 1 the value is one third minus one, which is minus two thirds. The whole region lies below the axis, so the area is the modulus of that, two thirds.
