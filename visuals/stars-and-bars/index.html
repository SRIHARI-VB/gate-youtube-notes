Stars and Bars (IODB)

Objective. See why n identical objects in k distinct boxes is C(n+k-1, k-1) = C(n+k-1, n), and what the bars physically are.
1.00x

Static explanation

Five identical sweets, three children. Because the sweets are identical, a distribution is fixed by the three counts (a, b, c) with a + b + c = 5 and each count at least 0.

Draw the five sweets as stars in a row, then put k - 1 = 2 bars somewhere in the row to separate child A's sweets from B's from C's. The arrangement **|***| is the distribution (2, 3, 0).

Every distribution gives exactly one arrangement of 5 stars and 2 bars, and every such arrangement gives exactly one distribution, so the counts are equal. The number of arrangements is the number of ways to choose which 2 of the 5 + 2 = 7 positions hold bars: C(7, 2) = 21.

If every child must get at least one sweet, hand out one sweet each first and distribute the remaining 2 freely: C(2 + 3 - 1, 3 - 1) = C(4, 2) = 6.

Common misconception

Stars and bars does not handle upper bounds. If the question caps each box (say every child gets at most 2), the formula silently over-counts. For 5 sweets, 3 children, each at most 2, the answer is not 21 and not 6 - you need a substitution or an inclusion-exclusion correction. A cap always means the plain template no longer applies.

Quick check

How many ways can 5 identical sweets be distributed among 3 children if a child may receive nothing?

Answer

21, from C(5 + 3 - 1, 3 - 1) = C(7, 2). If instead the sweets were 5 distinct sweets the answer would be 3^5 = 243, which is the DODB template.
