Static explanation

Every balls-into-boxes question in the GATE syllabus sits in exactly one of these four cells, and each cell has one method. Decide the two axes first, then write the formula - the wording of the question is what tells you which cell you are in.

  • Distinct objects means the objects can be told apart (different balls, different people, different digits in fixed roles). Identical objects means only counts matter (identical sweets, identical balls, repeated letters of one kind).
  • Distinct boxes means the containers are labelled (children A, B, C; bags 1 to n; distinct bins). Identical boxes means they are not (groups, teams, unlabelled bins).
  • Default rule: if the question names no containers, they are identical. "Split 6 students into 3 groups" is DOIB, not DODB.
Common misconception

Assuming boxes are labelled because they are mentioned, or assuming objects are distinct because the sentence uses the plural. Six identical balls into three identical bins is 7 (GATE CS 2022 Q.32); the same balls into three distinct bins would be C(8,2) = 28.

Quick check

Three identical chocolates into two named children, a child may get none. Which cell and what count?

Answer

IODB, 4 ways: (3,0), (2,1), (1,2), (0,3) - that is C(3 + 2 - 1, 2 - 1) = C(4,1) = 4. If the chocolates were distinct it would be DODB: 2^3 = 8.
