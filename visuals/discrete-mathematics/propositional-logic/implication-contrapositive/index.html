Converse, Inverse and Contrapositive

Objective. Tell apart the three rewrites of p -> q, and see from the columns which one is actually equivalent.
1.00x

Static explanation

Four statements are built from p -> q. The original is p -> q. The converse swaps the parts: q -> p. The inverse negates both parts: not p -> not q. The contrapositive swaps and negates: not q -> not p.

Only the contrapositive is logically equivalent to the original, and the truth table shows it directly: the two columns are identical in all four rows. Converse and inverse are equivalent to each other, because each is the contrapositive of the other, but neither is equivalent to the original.

This matters in English. “If it is raining then the ground is wet” is true, and so is its contrapositive “if the ground is not wet then it is not raining”. Its converse, “if the ground is wet then it is raining”, can be false - a sprinkler also wets the ground.

Translation rules worth keeping beside this lesson: p only if q is p -> q; p if q is q -> p; p unless q is not q -> p; q is necessary for p is p -> q.

Common misconception

The converse error: assuming q -> p from p -> q. In the table the two columns disagree at (p true, q false) and (p false, q true). A statement and its converse are two different claims, and only one of them was given to you.

Quick check

Which of the four statements is equivalent to p -> q?

Answer

The contrapositive, not q -> not p. Its column matches p -> q in every row. The converse and the inverse each differ in two rows.
