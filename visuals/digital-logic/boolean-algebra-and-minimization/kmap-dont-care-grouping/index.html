Don't-Care Grouping on a Karnaugh Map

Objective. Take one four-variable function - 1s at 0, 5, 10 and 15, don't cares at 2, 7, 8 and 13 - and watch the two quads appear. The order is the lesson: number the cells from the Gray-code labels, place the 1s, place the Xs, let an X join a group, read the variables that never change, then compare the cost with the answer you get by ignoring the don't cares.
1.00x

Static explanation

A four-variable map has sixteen cells, and the row and column labels are in Gray code: PQ runs 00, 01, 11, 10 and RS runs 00, 01, 11, 10. That ordering is what makes neighbours differ in exactly one bit, and it is why the third cell of the top row is minterm 3 while minterm 2 sits to its right. Always number a cell from its labels, never from its position on the page.

The function has four required 1s, at minterms 0, 5, 10 and 15. In binary they are 0000, 0101, 1010 and 1111, and no two of them differ in a single bit: 0 and 5 differ in two positions, 0 and 10 in two, 5 and 15 in two, 0 and 15 in four. So without help every 1 is a group of one cell, and the sum of products is the canonical form of four 4-literal terms, sixteen literals in all.

The question also supplies four don't cares, at 2, 7, 8 and 13. A don't care is a row whose output does not matter, so it may be taken as 1 whenever that enlarges a group - and it never has to be covered. Using 2 and 8, the corners 0, 2, 8 and 10 become a group of four: P and R take both values inside it while Q stays 0 and S stays 0, so the term is Q'S'. Using 7 and 13, the cells 5, 7, 13 and 15 become a second group of four: Q and S stay 1, so the term is QS.

The minimal sum of products is therefore Q'S' + QS, which is Q XNOR S: two terms and four literals, against four terms and sixteen literals for the canonical form. The don't cares were used inside the groups and excluded from the list of required 1s, and that is the rule to carry into the exam.

Common misconception

Two mistakes dominate this question. Treating a don't care as a 1 that must be covered, which makes the answer larger instead of smaller; and grouping only along the drawn edges, which loses the corner quad, because a Karnaugh map wraps around - the left edge touches the right and the top touches the bottom. A third: reading the term from the position of the group rather than from the variables that stay constant, which flips Q'S' into QS.

Quick check

Which cells of this map are used by the group QS, and how many of them are don't cares?

Answer

Minterms 5, 7, 13 and 15, of which 7 and 13 are don't cares. All four have Q = 1 and S = 1 while P and R take both values, so the term keeps Q and S only. The two required 1s in the group are 5 and 15; the other two cells were borrowed from the don't-care list.
